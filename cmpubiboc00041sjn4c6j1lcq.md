Earlier this year I stood in front of a group of learners in Port Harcourt and taught them how to use Artificial Intelligence tools in their everyday work and business. Not theory. Not robotics. Not machine learning algorithms. Practical, usable AI — the kind that saves you three hours of work every day if you know how to use it properly. I did this as a partner tutor at TechBarr Digital Academy, and what I observed in that class changed how I think about digital education in Nigeria. The Problem With How AI Is Being Taught in Nigeria Most AI content online is written for developers in San Francisco. It assumes you have a computer science degree, a fast laptop, and a dollar card to pay for subscriptions. The average Nigerian professional has none of those things. What they have is a smartphone, a business to run, limited data, and zero time to figure out complex tools on their own. That gap between how AI is taught globally and what Nigerian professionals actually need is exactly what I set out to close in that classroom. What I Actually Taught The curriculum I developed covered three core areas:

1.  Understanding What AI Can and Cannot Do Most people either think AI is magic that will do everything for them, or they think it is a dangerous technology that will replace their jobs. Neither is true. AI is a tool. Like Microsoft Word is a tool. The people who learn to use it well will have a serious advantage over those who do not — in the same way that knowing how to type fast gave people an advantage in the 1990s.
    
2.  The CRAFT Prompt Framework The single most important skill for using AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini is knowing how to give them instructions. Most people type one line and get a mediocre result. Then they conclude that AI is not useful. The CRAFT framework I taught stands for:
    

C — Context: tell the AI who you are and what situation you are in R — Role: tell the AI what role to play A — Action: tell the AI exactly what you want it to do F — Format: tell the AI how to present the output T — Tone: tell the AI how to sound

A prompt using CRAFT gets dramatically better results than a one-line question. I showed learners this live in the class — the difference was immediate and visible. 3. Practical Tools for Nigerian Professionals I walked through specific tools that work well even with limited internet and without a dollar card:

ChatGPT free tier — for writing, research, brainstorming Claude — for longer documents, detailed analysis Canva AI features — for graphics and presentations Google Gemini — integrated into Gmail and Google Docs Otter.ai — for meeting transcription Notion AI — for note-taking and organisation

For each tool I showed a real use case relevant to Nigerian business — writing proposals, creating social media content, drafting emails, summarising long documents. What the Learners Said The most common reaction after the class was this: "I did not know AI could do this. I have been doing this manually for years." That sentence tells you everything about the opportunity and the gap that exists in Nigeria right now. Business owners who learn to use AI tools properly can produce better proposals, respond to clients faster, create more content, and make better decisions — all without hiring extra staff. That is not hype. That is what I watched happen in real time in that classroom. Who Should Learn This If you fall into any of these categories, AI literacy is no longer optional for you:

You run a small or medium business in Nigeria You work in marketing, sales, or client services You are a freelancer or consultant You are a student preparing to enter the workforce You are a professional who writes documents, emails, or reports regularly
